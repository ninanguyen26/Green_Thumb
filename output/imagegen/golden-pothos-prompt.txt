Create eight coordinated GOLDEN POTHOS plant sprites for a cozy plant-shop game.

PLANT
Common name: Golden pothos
Scientific name: Epipremnum aureum
Form: Standard golden-variegated houseplant.

Botanical features:
A trailing vine with alternating, pointed, heart-shaped leaves. Leaves have
a shallow notch at the base and irregular golden-yellow streaks and patches
over medium-green foliage. Slender green stems connect the leaves at visible
nodes. Keep the familiar indoor form: entire, unbroken leaves with no splits
or holes. No flowers, climbing pole, or trellis.

The youngest plant has fewer, smaller leaves and a compact habit. As it grows,
develop longer vines and more foliage. The mature plant has a full central
cluster with several gracefully trailing vines. Preserve recognizable golden
variegation across all stages.

STYLE
Use the attached images as drawing-style references:
warm, charming hand-drawn illustration; slightly irregular medium-brown
outlines; rounded organic shapes; muted sage, olive, and medium greens;
soft golden-yellow variegation; restrained warm cream highlights.
Simple flat colors with one subtle shadow tone.

Match the readable flower illustration in the bouquet reference and the
warm palette of the streetscape reference. Do not use the streetscape’s
isometric perspective.

Keep silhouettes and individual leaves readable at a small game size.
No photorealism, glossy 3D rendering, harsh black outlines, gradients,
or intricate leaf-vein detail.

VIEW AND COMPOSITION
Show isolated plants from the soil surface upward, viewed from the front
with a slight view from above. Trailing vines may hang below the soil-surface
anchor into transparent space.

No pot, soil, exposed underground roots, ground, background, cast shadow,
text, labels, borders, or interface elements. Transparent background.

Use the same camera angle, lighting, outline thickness, and botanical
identity throughout. Arrange exactly eight sprites in a four-column,
two-row sheet with equal-sized cells and generous transparent gutters.

Place the central stem-base / soil-surface anchor at exactly the same
position in every cell, horizontally centered and approximately 65% down.
Reserve the space below for trailing vines. Keep all foliage inside its
cell with transparent padding.

Keep a consistent world scale: sprouts should actually be smaller,
not enlarged to fill their cells.

TOP ROW — LEFT TO RIGHT

1. SPROUT — HEALTHY
A small established golden pothos juvenile with a short stem and two or
three small, pointed, heart-shaped leaves. Fresh green foliage with a few
subtle golden-yellow markings. Compact, upright, lively posture.
Draw recognizable young pothos foliage, not generic seedling cotyledons.

2. SPROUT — HALF DEAD
The same juvenile as sprite 1, with corresponding stems and leaves.
Leaves hang limply and curl inward slightly. One leaf has a dry brown tip
and edge; foliage is duller, but some living green remains.
Keep natural golden variegation distinct from brown damage.
Visibly distressed but still alive.

3. GROWING — HEALTHY
An intermediate golden pothos with approximately six to eight heart-shaped
leaves on a few developing vines. A modest central cluster with one or two
short vines beginning to trail. Medium-green foliage with irregular golden
patches and streaks. Fuller than the sprout but clearly smaller than mature.

4. GROWING — THIRSTY
The same plant structure and leaf arrangement as sprite 3.
Leaf stalks and vine tips droop; leaves hang lower and curl inward gently.
Foliage remains mostly green with its golden markings intact.
Little or no brown damage and no major leaf loss.
Clearly thirsty, but readily recoverable.

BOTTOM ROW — LEFT TO RIGHT

5. GROWING — HALF DEAD
The same underlying plant as sprite 3.
More pronounced drooping, limp vine tips, curled leaves, and several dry
brown tips or edges. A small amount of foliage loss exposes a few nodes.
Retain living green leaves and recognizable golden variegation.
Clearly more distressed than the thirsty version, but still alive.

6. MATURE — HEALTHY
A lush, shop-ready golden pothos with approximately twelve to sixteen
readable heart-shaped leaves. A full central cluster and several vines
trailing naturally outward and down on both sides, with slight asymmetry.
Leaves alternate along the vines and vary naturally in size and orientation.
Rich green foliage with irregular golden-yellow marbling.
No split leaves, holes, flowers, or climbing support.

7. MATURE — THIRSTY
The same mature plant as sprite 6, preserving its vines, leaf arrangement,
and overall scale. Leaf stalks sag, leaves hang limply and curl slightly,
and trailing vine tips lose their lively curve.
Keep most foliage green, with golden variegation intact and minimal damage.
Do not shrink the plant or remove large amounts of foliage.

8. MATURE — HALF DEAD
The same underlying mature plant as sprite 6.
Pronounced wilting, curled and limp leaves, several brown tips and patches,
and modest foliage loss that exposes sections of vine.
Trailing stems remain attached and visibly alive.
Retain enough green foliage to communicate that recovery is possible.
Natural golden-yellow variegation must remain distinguishable from damage.

CONSISTENCY
Condition variants must look like the same individual plant changing health,
not different specimens. Preserve corresponding stems, leaves, and
variegation patterns wherever they remain present.

Growth stages should develop through additional leaves and longer vines,
not by simply scaling the same drawing.

Do not include recovering or gone sprites; recovery will be animated in code.
Do not draw warning icons, water droplets, sparkles, faces, or decorative props.
